Android 16 (crDroid) for the **Huawei MateBook E Go** (Snapdragon 8cx Gen 3 /
SC8280XP) on a **mainline Linux kernel**, with hardware Vulkan on the Adreno 690.

This release brings a **new hardware video decoder driver** that handles stopping,
seeking and replaying reliably, a **working built-in microphone**, the rear camera's
**autofocus**, a **speaker enhancement** you can switch on — and the first preview
of a **graphical installer** that can install next to Windows.

**Talk to us:** [Telegram](https://t.me/gaokunAndroid) · QQ group **920133252**

> ### ⚠️ Alpha
> Installing **erases the internal disk, Windows included**, and no image of the
> factory state exists anywhere. You should be comfortable recovering a machine
> that will not boot. No warranty.
>
> Any BIOS version works (the 2.16-only rule has been lifted). Secure Boot must be off.
> Instructions: **[docs/INSTALL.md](https://github.com/vahiru/gaokun-android/blob/main/docs/INSTALL.md)**
>
> Coming from **v0.2.x – v0.6.2**: update in Settings, nothing else to do.
> (v0.6.3 was never released; everything planned for it is in this release.)

## Video

**Hardware video decoding moved to the `qcom-iris` driver**, replacing `qcom-venus`,
which was unreliable on this machine. Along the way we fixed three places where
this machine's (Huawei-signed) video firmware and the upstream driver disagree:

* **Stopping a video mid-stream** no longer hangs the video firmware (hardware
  decoding used to stay dead until a reboot).
* **Seeking** no longer crashes the firmware (same consequence).
* **Seeking after the end of a video** (replaying it) shows the picture again, and
  every later end of stream is reported.

Tested with H.264, HEVC and VP9, including mid-stream resolution changes, and the
video block still powers down between videos. **VP8 is no longer hardware
decoded**: the new driver does not support it on this chip, and VP8 video now uses
the software decoder, which handles it easily.

## Microphone

**The built-in microphones record.** Recording used to produce empty files. The
audio HAL now honours the microphone the audio policy picks, captures at the rate
the hardware actually runs at, and hands captured audio over as soon as it arrives
— so recordings no longer start with a fraction of a second of silence and no
longer lag by ~120 ms. Thanks to **@mashen11** (PR #10).

## Speaker enhancement (experimental)

An optional sound processing chain for the built-in speakers, based on the
Huawei Histen processing the Windows driver uses, tuned against measurements of
the Windows audio by **@mashen11** (PR #7, #8). It is **off by default**: *Settings › Sound ›
Speaker enhancement (experimental)*; switching it on takes effect from the next
playback. Only the built-in speakers are processed — headphones, Bluetooth and
USB audio are passed through untouched.

## Graphical installer (preview)

A touch-friendly installer with a Material Design 3 interface, running from its
own small Linux system. What it adds over the command-line installer:

* **Dual boot**: install into free space next to Windows (or shrink a partition
  first — it refuses a hibernated or Fast-Startup Windows volume, which would
  get corrupted).
* **No USB stick needed**: from Windows, `gaokun3-setup.cmd` puts the installer
  on the internal disk — taking only the half a gigabyte or so it needs — and
  boots into it once; you make room for Android in the installer.
* **Reinstall Android** in place, wiping data by default or keeping it.
* **Adjust the disk** by hand, and **download the system over Wi-Fi** instead of
  carrying it on the medium.

It is a **preview**: reinstalling (keeping data, from the medium and over the
network) has been done on hardware; dual boot, erasing the whole disk, the
Windows script and booting from a USB stick have only run on test disks and a
virtual machine so far. The command-line installer remains the tested path.
Details: [docs/INSTALL.md](https://github.com/vahiru/gaokun-android/blob/main/docs/INSTALL.md#graphical-installer-preview).
Reports are very welcome — include the version from the bottom of the side bar
and `gaokun3/diag/installer.log` from the medium.

## Camera

**The rear camera focuses.** It has a voice-coil motor that nothing drove until
now. The camera HAL runs contrast-detect autofocus on the frames itself (the
software ISP produces no focus statistics): continuous focus in preview, and a
tap or the shutter button locks focus. The focus state reaches apps the way
Camera2 specifies, so apps that wait for "focus locked" before taking the
picture no longer time out. Front camera: fixed focus, as the hardware is.
Thanks to **@mashen11** for the motor bring-up and calibration (PR #6).

**Photos are the right way up.** The HAL now applies the rotation the app asks
for (`JPEG_ORIENTATION`) to the pixels. Previously every photo came out in the
sensor's native orientation regardless of how the tablet was held.

**Rotating the tablet no longer crashes the camera.** Reconfiguring the session
(which apps do on rotation or when switching cameras) deadlocked the HAL.

## USB

**USB adb survives unplugging and replugging.** After any USB role change the
controller was left unable to start the device side again — which is why
reconnecting the cable used to need a reboot. The controller now uses the USB 2
clock it actually has (the USB 3 PHY clock can belong to DisplayPort), and the
data role follows what is really on the other end: a computer enumerates us as
a device; a hub or a flash drive gets us as the host. One case is not fixed yet:
after the tablet has been in standby, the port can still end up in that state —
see *Known issues*.

## Smaller things

* **The device declares itself a tablet** (`ro.build.characteristics=tablet`).
  Apps that choose a tablet layout or tablet login from this — QQ, for one — now
  do. ([#5](https://github.com/vahiru/gaokun-android/issues/5))
* **Faster downloads on high-latency connections**, including the in-system
  update: Wi-Fi TCP buffers were Android's defaults, which cap a single
  connection at about 3.5 MB/s when the server is ~300 ms away. Measured on the
  same link: 3.5 → 9.5 MB/s.
* Fresh installs made with `install-gaokun3.sh` now get the full kernel command
  line from `boot.img`; installs of v0.6.2 made that way were missing the touch
  contact-size axes until their first update.
* **The clock syncs in mainland China**: besides `time.android.com` (unreachable
  there) the system now also asks Aliyun, Tencent and the National Time Service
  Center.
* The device's own settings pages (*Detachable keyboard*, *Speaker enhancement*)
  now look like the rest of Settings, with the new Material 3 Expressive
  switches.
* SELinux: another round of rules and labels towards enforcing, from a full boot
  audit and an enforcing trial run (still `permissive`).
* Standby stays **on** by default, as before. The switch was renamed: if you had
  turned standby off with `persist.gaokun3.allow_suspend=0`, set
  `adb shell setprop persist.vendor.gaokun3.allow_suspend 0` again.

## Known issues

* SELinux is still `permissive`.
* No auto-brightness. The light sensor now answers on the bus, but refuses to
  start; we need a copy of the sensor registry from a machine that still has
  Windows — if that is you, please get in touch.
* Audio (and Bluetooth) can deadlock after long uptime; a watchdog records the
  evidence under `/data/vendor/gaokun3/hangdump-*` — please attach it if you hit
  this.
* A palm resting on the screen fragments into several touches.
* Autofocus in the dark is slow to settle (the only focus signal is image
  sharpness, and dark frames are mostly noise).
* Hardware video **encoding** is still off (apps record with the software encoder).
* If the video firmware ever does crash, hardware decoding may stay unavailable
  until a reboot (the driver's firmware restart is not always successful).
* **After standby, the USB-C port can stop working** (no USB adb, no USB devices)
  until a reboot; charging is not affected. It happens on some wake-ups, not all:
  plugging the cable back in after standby worked in one of our two tests and not
  in the other. The detachable keyboard is on a different controller and keeps
  working.

update. For a **fresh install** you want `super.img.zst` and `boot.img`.
